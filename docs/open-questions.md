# Open questions

Residual uncertainty, **not pending work**. Nothing here is a bug and nothing here is a task list: each entry records a shape that has not been measured, or a measurement that covers its range by construction rather than end to end. Read the relevant entry before claiming a shape is handled.

Four of these carry a prohibition that fires mid-edit with no trigger phrase, so those prohibitions are stated in the Hard rules in `CLAUDE.md` as well. The evidence behind each one is here.

- **No Chromium floor has been established.** Firefox 126 is the only floor stated anywhere. The style is known to work in one ungoogled-chromium build (2026-09-10) and nothing narrower has been checked. `README.md` and `USw-notes.md` both now say as much — not restricted, confirmed once, no floor, treat as lightly tested — so a Chromium user is no longer left with nothing. What is still open is whether to claim support properly, which needs a version actually tested.
- An inherited `:not(.xtcbf50)` was removed from the two-column selector. The class was absent everywhere checked, live and saved, so no offline test can validate the removal — restore it first if a post type ever lays out wrongly.
- The post-page **column widths** were measured against one aspect ratio each. Both 3:4 carousel snapshots hold media 449px wide, so 3:4 is still the only shape `--u-post-width` has been measured against in numbers. Its *reach* widened on 2026-09-14: the user checked a **4:5 carousel live** on the post and reel pages and reported it responding to the setting, which only a column carrying an inline `--x-maxWidth` can do — so 4:5 sits on the token side of the boundary alongside 3:4, and the setting is now labelled "portrait carousel" rather than "3:4 carousel post". What stays open is that this was confirmed by response rather than by measurement, that no snapshot of a 4:5 carousel exists, and that no **9:16 carousel** has been found anywhere at all, so "portrait" is an honest name for two ratios rather than a range checked end to end. Every other carousel measured has no `--x-maxWidth` and is capped by `--u-post-photo-width` instead. The defaults are picked compromises, not derived numbers: do not write arithmetic justifying one, and do not treat a default as evidence of anything.
- **On the POST and REEL pages, a portrait reel that is not 9:16 is the one reel shape still sized by guesswork.** Landscape reels were the gap until 2026-09-12: four ratios — `56.2696%`, `75%`, `69.4981%`, `88.8614%` — were reported live as responding to nothing but the text settings, then captured, and the cause measured. Their column carries **no inline `--x-maxWidth`**, and every reel rule was keyed on that token. They are now caught by `.xvc5jky:not([style*="--x-maxWidth"]):has(video)` and all four measure correctly, and since 2026-09-14 that rule reads a setting of its own, `--u-reel-landscape`, rather than sharing `--u-post-photo-width` with carousels and single photos. What remains is the other side of the boundary, and it narrowed on 2026-09-14: the user checked **3:4 reels live on both the reel and the post pages** and reports them responding to the portrait-reel settings as expected, which is what carrying the token predicts and is the first evidence of any kind that a sub-9:16 portrait reel takes this path rather than the landscape one. The arithmetic was measured the same day and behaves exactly as designed: at a 1638px viewport with the height at 1200 and the width not binding, the 3:4 media came out 674×898.667 against a predicted 675×900 — `1200 * 9/16 + 380` = 1055 of column, 675 of media, `675 * 4/3` of height. The general form is `H * 9/16 * R`, so a source of ratio R reaches that fraction of its budget: exact at 9:16, three quarters at 3:4, 70% at 4:5. What is left is therefore not uncertainty about behaviour but a deliberate imprecision, plus the gap that no snapshot exercises this path at all. A census of every inline `padding-bottom` box across all twenty-one snapshots found no `video` in any 133% box on any page, so a 3:4 reel has never been **captured** anywhere — the 2026-09-14 check was live, and a snapshot of one would still be worth having; on the feed it could not be captured at all, since Instagram flattens anything taller than 4:5 to 125%. The **feed** has no equivalent gap — since 2026.9.11.7 a non-125% feed reel is sized from the video's intrinsic dimensions, which needs no ratio matching at all. See the note in `docs/rule-notes-feed.md` before touching that one: it cannot be measured offline.
- **The feed's side-crop limitation is real, and the shape it bites is 3:4 rather than the 4:5 the note was written for.** Instagram flattens everything taller than 4:5 into the 125% box, 3:4 included (`docs/rule-notes-feed.md` records 3:4 sources rendering at 125%, not their natural 133.33%), so every source between 4:5 and 9:16 arrives indistinguishable from a 9:16 one and is treated as 9:16. On 2026-09-14 the user reported having written the original limitation while taking 3:4 for 4:5, and having since seen a creator who posts consistently in 3:4 — so the affected set is **not** empty, and the confirmed member of it is 3:4. Whether a genuinely 4:5 video exists at all is still unknown and no longer the point.

  The cost is computable from the measurements in `docs/rule-notes-feed.md`. Past the knee the box is a true 9:16, so a source of ratio R loses `1 - R / 1.7778` of its width: 25% for 3:4, 29.7% for 4:5. At the default, box 550x858.8 at a 1638px window, a 3:4 source scales to 644.1 wide to cover and loses 94.1px, about 15%. The crop therefore exists at the default, not only at small width settings — the setting decides how much.

  **Nothing in CSS can separate them**, which is why this is recorded here rather than fixed. Feed `<video>` elements carry no dimensions in the markup (`class`, `playsinline`, `preload`, `poster="data:,"` and nothing else), the box is Instagram's clamp rather than a computed ratio, and CSS cannot branch on intrinsic dimensions. The one lever that renders them differently without distinguishing them is `object-fit: contain` on the 125% video, which trades the crop for banding on both shapes; it was put to the user on 2026-09-14 with the numbers and declined — no rule changes. A near-4:5 or near-3:4 source whose box carries decimals is a different case and is already handled: it fails the anchored `125%` match and falls to the family that sizes from intrinsic dimensions.

- **A SQUARE reel has never been captured, and the landscape-reel setting would catch one.** `--u-reel-landscape` is applied by `.xvc5jky:not([style*="--x-maxWidth"]):has(video)`, whose guard is not "landscape" but "no inline `--x-maxWidth`" -- which per the boundary this block turns on means square OR landscape. So a 1:1 reel would take the setting labelled "total width of a landscape reel and caption". The label is the honest name for every shape actually measured: a census across all twenty-one snapshots found the rule matching four reels, at `56.2696%`, `69.4981%`, `75%` and `88.8614%`, and no reel at `100%` anywhere. Whether Instagram even serves a 1:1 reel is unestablished. If one is ever captured, the question is only whether the label should widen -- the sizing would be correct either way, since the rule caps the column and lets Instagram fill it, and reads no ratio at all.
- **A 9:16 single photo is handled on the feed and untested on a post page.** `Instagram_feed2.html` article 10 is a sponsored photo post in a `padding-bottom:177.8%` box with no `video` anywhere in the article — so Instagram does accept a photo at that ratio, and the feed's shape-agnostic photo rules size it like any other. On a post page the width cap's leading-digit guard would cap it, since `177` starts with `1`, but no post-page snapshot of one exists. That same box is also why the reel block stays URL-scoped: `main div[style*="padding-bottom"][style*="177"]` matches it on the saved feed.
- **The landscape range is now measured at seven points, all of them below 100%.** Photos at `56.4394%` and `75%`, a carousel at `75%`/`74.9712%`, and reels at `56.2696%`, `69.4981%`, `75%` and `88.8614%`. Every one of them has no inline `--x-maxWidth`, which is what the leading-digit guard and the `:not([style*="--x-maxWidth"])` guards both depend on. The 52.36–99.99% range is still covered by construction rather than end to end, and the closest capture to the 100% boundary is 88.8614%; nothing at 1.91:1 exists.
- **Instagram re-derives a carousel slide's width from its container — confirmed live on 2026-09-11, and the reason a snapshot will always disagree.** Each slide frame carries an inline literal `width:<n>px` written by Instagram's JS, with the slide offsets as multiples of it; SingleFile freezes both. So on the saved square-carousel page the cap narrows the container while the slide stays 1262, and any offline measurement reports a 493px overflow. That is the capture, not the page: live, the slides resize correctly at every `--u-post-photo-width` value and each lands accurately in the frame, the same way they do on unstyled Instagram when the viewport is resized. Treat an overflow reported against this snapshot as expected, and do not add a zoom or a derived-scale rule to correct it.

- **Before reaching for `zoom` on a carousel, check how the slide's width is written** — the feed and the post pages differ, and both were read live in DevTools on 2026-09-11. On the **feed** the div inside each `li` carries `width: calc(-2px + min(470px, 100vw))`, a static CSS expression that references nothing we touch, so the slide stays 468px and `zoom` scales it cleanly; the `translateX` offsets are JS-written px but are multiples of that same fixed width. On a **post page** the same div carries a literal `width:1262px` that Instagram's JS derived from the container, so widening the container made it recompute and the zoom cancelled exactly — which is why that rule and `--u-post-media-scale` were removed in 2026.9.11.4 while the feed's `--u-carousel-scale-max` was kept. Feed media is served at full resolution (a slide measured 3277×4096 in a 468px box), so scaling it up is not upscaling.

- A **mixed carousel** (photo plus video slides) on a `/p/` URL remains uncaptured, but one now exists on the **feed** — `Instagram_feed2.html` article 7, three slides, two with `video` and one with `img`, all in 125% boxes inside `li`. The feed reel rules miss it on the `a[href*="/reels/"]` guard and the feed photo rules on `:not(li *)`, so it is handled by the carousel zoom alone. On a post page such a carousel with no token matches two rules at once, the slide-list cap and the `:has(video)` cap. Those set the same property to the same value until 2026-09-14, when the `:has(video)` cap was given its own setting; they now set different values, and the slide-list cap wins on specificity (0,3,4) against (0,2,4), so a mixed carousel takes the carousel setting rather than the landscape-reel one. That was measured in Firefox on a synthetic mixed carousel, not on a capture -- the overlap still matches 0 on all twenty-one snapshots. See `docs/removed.md` for what to try first if one lays out wrongly.
